# Talk to bachelor biology students

## Goal
Give a talk to ~10 bachelors students aroundDevelopment of computational method to analyse data.
To cover: Why we need bioinformatics? What problems it addresses, and how we develop tools to do so?

## Students previous background
Our group consists of third-year bachelor students in Biomedical Sciences, as well as master’s students from various programmes within the Graduate School of Life Sciences in Utrecht. As a result, the level of knowledge is somewhat heterogeneous: master’s students tend to have more specialised expertise, while bachelor students have a broader, but generally less in-depth, background. That said, all students should be able to follow the core concepts of the topics you mentioned. 
Regarding specific areas:
Oncology: All students have had basic oncology. The depth of knowledge varies depending on elective courses taken during the bachelor.
Gene expression profiling: Students are familiar with interpreting outputs such as heatmaps and volcano plots, as this is covered in multiple bachelor courses.
Sequencing techniques: Common sequencing methods have been covered in depth during several courses, and students are generally comfortable with the underlying principles.
Transcriptomics (including single-cell and spatial): RNA sequencing is covered in the bachelor, including general concepts and applications. While students are familiar with the pipeline of data-processing, they will not all be able to fully explain each step in detail. During these courses, the emphasis has been on the methodology and interpreting results.

## Topics of the talk

1. My CV and my academic and professional story. Goal: show how I ended up being a bioinformatician and what steps in the career are needed. (see docs/CV_JosepGarnicaCaparros_short.txt). I started as wet lab but also as transcriptomic data produced in my lab piled up, during the covid lock down I started learning bioinformatics to analyse it.
2. First biological problem solved by bioinformatics ("crisis of too much biological data."). Protein sequence analysis in the 1960s–70s
3. Explore the curse of dimensionality and introduction to PCA
4. How we annotate cell type in scRNA-seq data. Introduction into scGate (https://github.com/carmonalab/scGate) and ProjecTILs (https://github.com/carmonalab/ProjecTILs).
5. Current challenges and future bioinformatic tools: address AI advantatges and limitations.
